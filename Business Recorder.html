SINGAPORE: Oil prices fell on Wednesday on data showing an increase in US crude and fuel inventories last week, although ongoing US-Iran attacks and disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz limited losses.

Brent crude futures fell 61 cents, or 0.7%, to $92.53 a barrel at 0550 GMT.

US West Texas Intermediate futures lost $1.24, or 1.4%, to $84.77 a barrel.

“Crude is wedged between a persistent geopolitical risk premium from tanker attacks and escalating tit-for-tat US-Iran strikes, and an increasingly bearish sentiment from a chorus of analysts forecasting a sizeable market surplus,” said Vandana Hari, founder and CEO at Vanda Insights. Meanwhile, the price spread between Brent and WTI has also widened in recent sessions, with WTI seen logging steeper declines during Wednesday trading.

The prompt Brent/WTI spread was hovering at a more than two-week high, LSEG data showed.

Araghchi warns states against aiding attacks on Iran

“WTI arbitrage trades have been shut to Asia and North West Europe, thus WTI correcting more than Brent is the expected price movement to allow the arbitrage window to open again,” said June Goh, senior analyst at Sparta Commodities.

This is particularly so with crude stocks building in the US Gulf Coast, Goh added.

US crude stocks rose by 5.28 million barrels in the week ended November 13, market sources said on Tuesday, citing data from the American Petroleum Institute.

Gasoline stocks rose by 5.12 million barrels and distillate inventories by 351,000 barrels.

But continued tensions between the US and Iran and ongoing risks to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz kept price declines limited. Bahrain’s defence force said on Wednesday that it had intercepted Iranian aerial attacks on the country, which hosts the US Navy’s Fifth Fleet, marking another escalation in the conflict a day after the US and Iran traded strikes.

Both oil benchmarks had settled higher on Tuesday as the US-Iran conflict intensified, even as talks continued between Iran and mediators Oman and Qatar.

Attacks on ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz also stoked continued supply worries.

Iranian drones attacked a US military radar system in Bahrain on Tuesday, and the US retaliated with strikes on a missile launch site on Iran’s Qeshm Island.

An LPG tanker was also struck by a projectile while sailing through the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Oman on Monday, and crew had to evacuate the ship.

“Although regional supply may continue to recover through alternative routes, the latest vessel data suggests that normalization of flows through Hormuz is still not possible under current conditions,” LSEG analysts said in a note on Tuesday.

Iran, Oman FMs discuss shipping, diplomacy

Yemen’s Houthis said on Tuesday they had launched five drones towards two pumping stations along Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, which it has used during the war to reroute oil to its Yanbu port for export, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz.

By BR Web Desk

Selling pressure continued at the Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX), with the benchmark KSE-100 Index settling with a loss of over 1,200 points on Wednesday.

The benchmark index remained under pressure throughout the session, with selling emerging shortly after the opening bell. It initially dropped sharply, followed by a brief recovery, before extending losses and touching an intraday low of 176,053.63.

The market attempted a recovery around midday, climbing towards the 176,700 level, but failed to sustain the momentum. The index subsequently came under renewed selling pressure during the second half of the session, retreating towards its intraday low before staging a modest recovery.

The KSE-100 touched an intraday high of 177,719.05 but remained unable to regain momentum, eventually settling in negative territory.

At close, the benchmark index settled at 176,294.33, down 1,223.40 points or 0.69%.

In a key development, the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) has cautioned that evolving external and domestic risks, particularly a prolonged conflict in the Middle East and extreme weather events associated with El Niño, could pose challenges to the country’s macroeconomic outlook and affect projected economic outcomes.

On Tuesday, PSX witnessed broad-based selling and lower trading activity as investors booked profits at higher levels, reversing an early rally supported by declining international oil prices. The benchmark KSE-100 Index fell by 665.21 points, or 0.37%, to close at 177,517.74 points.

Globally, stocks rose in Asia on Wednesday as Nvidia shrugged off recent turmoil to ​post a better-than-expected sales forecast, boosting companies in its hardware supply chain and lifting the regional benchmark ‌after four days of losses.

MSCI’s broadest index of Asia-Pacific shares outside Japan advanced 0.9%, led by a gain of as much as 3.3% for South Korea’s KOSPI.

Japan’s Nikkei 225 climbed 0.6%, while US S&P 500 e-mini futures were up 0.5%.

Nvidia shares rose 4.7% in after-hours trading after the ​chipmaker, seen as a bellwether for the AI sector, reported quarterly revenue above forecasts in its first earnings report since ​Jensen Huang’s abrupt departure as CEO this month.

The gains came after the Wall Street Journal ⁠reported the SEC is probing Nvidia, citing people familiar with the matter.

Overnight, stocks on Wall Street sold off again, with the S&P 500 down 1% to log a fourth consecutive session lower prior to Nvidia’s earnings report.
