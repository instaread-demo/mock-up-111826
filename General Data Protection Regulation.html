Preparing for the GRE can feel overwhelming, especially when you're staring down a wall of prep companies, apps, books, and courses that all promise to boost your score. To cut through the noise, this guide pulls directly from real student reviews across Reddit communities like r/GRE, r/GradSchool, r/PTschool, and r/AskAcademia. These are the voices of people who actually took the test, scored everywhere from the 290s to a stunning 336, and shared honestly about what worked and what wasted their money.

Below you'll find a quick summary, an explanation of what GRE prep resources actually are, how to use them effectively, and a detailed breakdown of the top options including their descriptions, features, and prices.

Quick Listing Summary

For readers who want the bottom line first, here are the most-recommended GRE prep resources according to Reddit users:

  • GregMat+ — Best overall value. Around $5–$8/month. Structured curriculum, video lessons, and a live instructor option. Repeatedly called “the GOAT.”
  • Manhattan 5 lb. Book of GRE Practice Problems — Best single book. Around $30–$40. Thousands of realistic practice questions.
  • Target Test Prep (TTP) — Best for building a Quant foundation from scratch. Around $500 for six months. Expensive but thorough.
  • Magoosh — Best for vocab and budget self-study. Free app version plus paid premium tiers. Great flashcards.
  • Official ETS Materials — Essential for realistic practice. Practice tests and guides roughly $100–$140. The only true-to-test questions.
  • Kaplan / Princeton Review / Manhattan Prep (live courses) — Best for accountability and live instruction. $200 to $1,400+. Mixed reviews on value.

What Are GRE Prep Resources?

GRE prep resources are the books, online courses, apps, and practice tests designed to help you prepare for the Graduate Record Examination, the standardized test required by many graduate, PhD, MBA, and professional programs. The GRE measures three areas: Quantitative Reasoning (math), Verbal Reasoning (reading and vocabulary), and Analytical Writing.

These resources generally fall into a few categories. Prep books are the traditional option, offering content review and large banks of practice problems. Online subscription platforms like GregMat and Magoosh combine video lessons, quizzes, flashcards, and study schedules into one account. Official ETS materials come straight from the test makers, so the questions mirror the real exam exactly. Finally, live or recorded courses from companies like Kaplan, Princeton Review, and Manhattan Prep offer structured instruction, often with a teacher and a fixed weekly schedule.

One important theme from Reddit is that the GRE is section-level adaptive, meaning the difficulty of your second section changes based on how you performed in the first. Several users noted that prep platforms mimicking this adaptive style feel much closer to the real test than static question banks, which can leave you surprised on test day.

How to Use GRE Prep Resources Effectively

The single most repeated piece of advice across every thread is this: the study plan matters more than the platform. As one parent researching for their daughter put it after weeks of digging through forums, a clear 90-day plan tends to work far better than stretching prep over six months with no structure.

Here is the general approach that successful Reddit users recommend:

  • Start with a baseline. Before buying anything fancy, take a free official ETS practice test to see where you stand. This tells you how far you are from your goal score and whether your foundations are weak or strong.
  • Build your foundation first. Especially for Quant, don't waste practice questions trying to solve hard problems before you understand basic algebra, geometry, and exponents. One user who scored 336 admitted he “did not know how to add fractions” when he started, so he rebuilt his math from the ground up.
  • Learn the approach, not just the content. A common tip is to study the strategy and timing approach, not only the math itself. Users emphasize being thorough and patient rather than rushing.
  • Drill by topic, then practice mixed sets. Work through one concept at a time until you hit around 90% accuracy, then move to full practice sections and timed tests.
  • Keep an error log. Nearly every high scorer mentions tracking mistakes and revisiting them. One user said a question similar to one they'd missed in practice appeared on the real exam.
  • Commit fully for about three months. As the 336 scorer warned, half-hearted studying spread across many months leads to forgetting and stress. Three months of focused, roughly four-hours-a-day effort works better.

A recurring caution: most paid programs make their practice questions harder than the actual test. Several users estimated GregMat's tests run about 35% harder than the real GRE. So if a problem feels impossibly difficult, you probably already know the concept and should move on.

Top GRE Prep Resources According to Reddit

1. GregMat+

Description: GregMat is consistently the most beloved resource across every thread. Run by tutor Greg (who himself scored a perfect 340 with a 6.0 in writing), it offers a structured curriculum with video lessons, tickbox quizzes, flashcards, practice tests, and study plans. Many users say it alone is enough to succeed.

Features:

  • Full video lessons for Quant and Verbal
  • Vocabulary flashcards and a “vocab mountain” study path
  • Topic-based quizzes and a customizable “super quiz” for weak areas
  • Built-in study schedules, including an “I'm overwhelmed” plan for beginners
  • A live and recorded instructor option through GregMat+ and an active Discord community for free accountability

Price: Roughly $5 to $8 per month. One user described it as “foregoing a morning coffee.” The 336 scorer repeatedly praised Greg for keeping the price near $5 when competitors charge far more.

Reddit verdict: Called “the GOAT” and “hands down the best.” A few users, however, noted Greg's teaching style didn't click for them, which is a reminder that even the best resource isn't perfect for everyone.

2. Manhattan 5 lb. Book of GRE Practice Problems

Description: If you buy only one book, Reddit overwhelmingly says make it this one. The “5 lb. Big Book” is a massive collection of practice problems that users say closely match what appears on the actual exam. The 336 scorer bought every book available and concluded, “Just get the Manhattan 5 lb. Big Book.”

Features:

  • Thousands of realistic practice questions across all topics
  • Full-length practice tests included with the book (one user found these “life changing”)
  • Topic-organized sections that pair well with online video lessons
  • Useful as a screen break alternative when you've been staring at a computer too long

Price: Approximately $30 to $40 for the full book.

Reddit verdict: A user who scored in the 90th percentiles credited the five practice tests in this book directly, noting that after the harder practice tests, the real GRE “seemed so easy in comparison.”

3. Target Test Prep (TTP)

Description: TTP is an online platform praised mainly for building a Quant foundation from absolute zero. It walks you gently through every math concept in a logical, step-by-step path. One user with only a single college math class found it “very comprehensive” for the math they needed.

Features:

  • Detailed, beginner-friendly Quant curriculum
  • Progress tracking and error-tracking tools
  • Review quizzes that reinforce each concept
  • Strong “medium” difficulty questions that users say are the most representative

Price: Around $500 for a six-month subscription, though deals occasionally appear. Reddit users repeatedly flag this as “staggering” and say the cost is hard to justify unless money is no object.

Reddit verdict: Excellent for total Quant beginners who need hand-holding, but weak on Verbal. Users note its hardest questions don't match the real GRE's style well, so it's best used to build foundations rather than as your only resource. A popular combo is “TTP for Quant, GregMat for Verbal.”

4. Magoosh

Description: Magoosh is the go-to budget and vocabulary resource, mentioned positively in nearly every thread. It's especially popular among students in fields like physical therapy and grad school who used the free app to study vocab a little each day. It also licenses official ETS questions.

Features:

  • Free vocabulary flashcard app, praised for daily bite-sized study
  • Video explanations for practice problems
  • Full-length practice tests and hundreds of questions per section
  • Study schedules and a free seven-day trial
  • An extensive word bank that teaches new vocabulary the ETS books don't cover

Price: A free app version is available. Premium subscriptions are paid, and one user mentioned buying it for around $149 for six months. Tutoring add-ons are also offered.

Reddit verdict: Many users scored well using Magoosh as their main resource, reporting results like 150V/153M with a 4.5 writing and 155V/148M with a 5 on writing. That said, the 336 scorer felt it wasn't worth paying for when GregMat costs so little, suggesting you save the free trial for the practice tests.

5. Official ETS Materials

Description: These come directly from the makers of the GRE, so the questions are the real thing. Multiple users consider the official ETS practice tests and guides essential, no matter what else you use. The bundle typically includes the Official Guide and value combo with old test questions.

Features:

  • Authentic retired GRE questions
  • Three official practice tests that best reflect real test difficulty
  • The Official Guide to the GRE General Test plus verbal and quant question booklets
  • A free online practice test option
  • The GRE Mentor self-paced tool (sold separately)

Price: The practice tests and guides run roughly $100, with the GRE Mentor tool around $100 and the official practice program about $140.

Reddit verdict: Users strongly recommend buying all three official practice tests to see exactly what the real questions look like. One person used the $100 GRE Mentor between two test attempts and scored 147Q/146V with a 3.5 in writing, which admissions counselors called solid. The consensus: buy the practice tests, but the pricier programs are optional.

6. Kaplan, Princeton Review, and Manhattan Prep (Live Courses)

Description: These name-brand companies offer structured live or recorded courses. Their main value is accountability, structure, and live instruction rather than realistic questions. Reviews are genuinely mixed.

Features:

  • Weekly class structure that paces your studying
  • Live or recorded lectures and instructor support
  • Content review and test-taking strategy
  • Manhattan Prep is often singled out for stronger Quant instruction and smarter instructors

Price: Ranges widely, from about $200 for a book set to $1,400 or more for full live courses.

Reddit verdict: Opinions split sharply. Some users call Kaplan and Princeton Review “a scam” because their questions don't match the real test and run easier than the actual GRE. Others found the structure genuinely helpful for staying paced and learning the test format. A former Kaplan instructor noted these courses help most for people who need live examples and aren't shy about asking questions. The repeated warning is that a $1,000+ course doesn't take the test for you, and disciplined self-studiers often score just as high.

Final Takeaways from Reddit

Three lessons rise above the rest. First, you can score extremely well on a tiny budget. The combination of GregMat (around $5/month), the Manhattan 5 lb. book (around $35), and official ETS practice tests (around $100) appears again and again as the winning, affordable formula. One user explicitly combined cheap tools to save over $1,000 compared to a live course.

Second, expensive live courses sell you accountability more than results. As one detailed reviewer put it, most people who take $1,000+ courses don't score higher than disciplined self-studiers. If you struggle with consistency, a live course or a tutor may be worth it. If you're self-motivated, you can pocket the savings.
