Blue-Chip Stock #10: H2O America (HTO)

  • Dividend History: 58 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 16.3%

H2O America, formerly SJW Group, operates regulated water and wastewater utilities in California, Connecticut, Maine, and Texas.

Essential services and regulated service territories support durable demand, while infrastructure investment expands the assets on which utilities can earn approved returns.

On October 26th, 2026, H2O America reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Revenue increased 4% to $250.2 million, while adjusted diluted earnings per share fell to $1.04 from $1.27.

Rate increases supported revenue, but higher costs and the larger share count weighed on per-share results.

The company narrowed standalone adjusted EPS guidance to $3.10-$3.16. Guidance including Quadvest, the pending Cibolo Valley acquisition, and related financing is lower at $2.91-$2.97.

Its revised 2026 capital plan is about $465 million, while the 2026-2030 plan remains $2.7 billion. Management retained its long-term 6%-8% adjusted EPS growth target.

Quadvest closed on October 1st for $542.7 million in cash. Its 62,200 active connections and substantial development pipeline expand H2O America’s Texas operations, but the transaction is initially dilutive.

On November 3rd, the company completed a $373.75 million equity-unit financing carrying a 7.75% annual distribution rate. The purchase contracts also create future common-share issuance obligations.

The $0.44 quarterly dividend, or $1.76 annualized, follows a 4.8% increase and 58 consecutive years of growth. It represents about 60% of the acquisition-inclusive guidance midpoint, with rate recovery and financing costs central to future coverage.

Blue-Chip Stock #9: Badger Meter (BMI)

  • Dividend History: 34 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 16.3%

Badger Meter provides smart water meters, flow-measurement equipment, software, and related communication technologies to utilities and industrial customers.

Its expanding installed base and recurring software and service revenue support attractive long-term economics, while municipal water infrastructure provides durable demand.

On October 22nd, 2026, Badger Meter reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Sales were $235.9 million, little changed year over year but up 6% sequentially.

Operating earnings increased 4% to $47.7 million, operating margin improved to 20.2%, and diluted EPS rose 4% to $1.24.

Gross margin expanded 120 basis points to 41.9%. Utility water sales were flat including UDlive; excluding the acquisition, they declined 3% year over year but improved 5% sequentially.

Flow-instrumentation sales grew 2%, while software revenue and spending controls helped offset uneven utility project deployments.

Management expects both sequential and year-over-year base-revenue improvement in the fourth quarter, with momentum continuing into 2027.

The OneNetwork software launch extends Badger Meter’s monitoring and analysis capabilities across water networks. Its UDlive acquisition also broadens the business into sewer monitoring.

The company repurchased about $55 million of shares during the quarter. The August dividend increase lifted the quarterly payment 10% to $0.44, marking 34 consecutive years of growth.

The low starting yield limits current income, but a modest payout and recurring software revenue support further increases. Project timing and electronics costs remain the principal near-term risks.

Blue-Chip Stock #8: Bank of Utica (BKUT)

  • Dividend History: 31 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 16.3%

Bank of Utica is a commercial bank based in Utica, New York. Its shares trade over the counter under BKUT and BKUTK; this ranking uses BKUT.

The bank holds an unusually large securities portfolio relative to its loan book, so investment valuations have a substantial influence on reported earnings.

Bank of Utica’s regulatory call report, filed October 27th for the period ending September 30th, 2026, showed nine-month net income of $40.5 million, up from $23.1 million a year earlier.

That result included $36.7 million of pre-tax unrealized gains on equity securities. Net interest income increased to $21.5 million from $18.6 million, while non-interest expense was $7.4 million.

At September-end, assets totaled $1.52 billion, net loans were $155.2 million, deposits were $1.08 billion, and equity was $397.1 million. The Tier 1 leverage ratio was 26.19%.

Our full-year EPS forecast is $185. The $19.50 annual dividend assumption represents a payout of about 11%, and the bank has increased its dividend for 31 consecutive years.

Strong capitalization and the low payout support dividend capacity. However, securities gains can reverse, management commentary is limited, and the OTC shares are highly illiquid. Its return estimate depends partly on a valuation recovery that may take time to emerge.

Blue-Chip Stock #7: Thomson Reuters (TRI)

  • Dividend History: 33 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 16.8%

Thomson Reuters provides information, software, and workflow tools to legal, tax, accounting, corporate, and news professionals.

Its subscription-heavy model generates recurring revenue, while trusted proprietary content is especially valuable as customers adopt artificial intelligence.

On November 3rd, 2026, Thomson Reuters reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Revenue increased 10% to $1.83 billion, with 10% organic growth.

Recurring revenue represented 88% of sales, while the Big 3 professional segments also grew 10% organically.

Adjusted EBITDA increased 10%, with a 35.5% margin, and adjusted EPS rose 14% to $0.81. Free cash flow fell 23% to $408 million as working capital, taxes, and capital expenditure weighed on cash conversion.

After selling 51% of Global Print to KKR for about $500 million on October 1st, management updated its continuing-business outlook to roughly 9.5% revenue growth, 8.5%-9.0% organic growth, and a 39% adjusted EBITDA margin.

The retained 49% stake preserves participation in the print business while the main company focuses more heavily on recurring professional software and AI-enabled workflows.

On November 11th, Thomson Reuters announced a new $500 million buyback program. Its new issuer bid permits up to seven million shares to be repurchased from November 13th, 2026 through November 12th, 2027.

The quarterly dividend is $0.655, or $2.62 annualized, following a 10% increase and 33 consecutive years of dividend growth. Subscription demand and cash conversion remain central to sustaining capital returns.

Blue-Chip Stock #6: Brown & Brown (BRO)

  • Dividend History: 33 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 16.9%

Brown & Brown is a diversified insurance broker offering property-and-casualty, employee-benefits, risk-management, and specialty insurance solutions.

Because brokers generally do not assume underwriting risk, the company can produce attractive cash flow while benefiting from insurance pricing and acquisition-led expansion.

On October 21st, the company raised its quarterly dividend 9% to $0.18, or $0.72 annualized, marking its 33rd consecutive annual increase.

The updated dividend represents about 16% of our $4.45 adjusted EPS forecast. That leaves substantial reinvestment capacity, although the return case still requires stronger organic growth and effective acquisition integration.

On October 26th, 2026, Brown & Brown reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Revenue increased 7.4% to $1.7 billion, with organic growth of 1.2%, or 1.5% including contingent commissions.

Adjusted EPS rose 1.9% to $1.07, while reported diluted EPS declined 1.5% to $0.67.

Adjusted EBITDAC increased 1.7% to $597 million, but the adjusted margin contracted to 34.6% from 36.6%. The enlarged business is growing, though underlying revenue momentum is modest and integration costs remain relevant.

The acquisition of Accession Risk Management and its Risk Strategies and One80 Intermediaries businesses has materially enlarged Brown & Brown.

The combined organization has more than 23,000 professionals across over 700 locations, broadening its specialty capabilities and distribution reach.

Blue-Chip Stock #5: Stepan Company (SCL)

  • Dividend History: 60 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 17.6%

Stepan Company manufactures specialty and intermediate chemicals for cleaning, personal care, agriculture, construction, and industrial applications.

Its Surfactants, Polymers, and Specialty Products businesses provide essential ingredients, although demand and margins remain sensitive to manufacturing activity and raw-material costs.

On October 28th, 2026, Stepan reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Revenue increased more than 17% to $692 million, while adjusted EPS rose to $0.94.

Organic sales volume increased 7%, and adjusted EBITDA rose 18% to $66.3 million, reflecting stronger demand, margin recovery, and Project Catalyst savings.

Cash from operations was $29.3 million, and capital spending was $21.1 million, producing $8.2 million of free cash flow. Working-capital requirements limited cash conversion, and quarter-end net debt was $536 million.

The quarter included a $5.6 million restructuring charge. Year-to-date restructuring charges reached $76.1 million, illustrating the cost of repositioning the business.

In October, Stepan completed a $28.5 million land sale near its Millsdale site, with proceeds earmarked for debt repayment.

Management continues to expect full-year adjusted EBITDA growth, positive free cash flow, and debt reduction.

The October dividend increase raised the quarterly payment 2.5% to $0.405, or $1.62 annually. The company describes this as its 59th consecutive year of increases.

Our $2.65 adjusted EPS forecast implies a payout near 61%. The expected return depends largely on earnings recovery and cost savings, rather than a large starting yield or a low current valuation.

Blue-Chip Stock #4: Stryker Corporation (SYK)

  • Dividend History: 32 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 17.6%

Stryker is a leading medical-technology company with products spanning MedSurg, Neurotechnology, and Orthopaedics.

Its installed base of surgical equipment, implants, instruments, and robotic systems creates recurring demand as procedure volumes grow.

On October 29th, 2026, Stryker reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Sales increased 7.7% to $6.5 billion, including 7.7% organic growth.

Organic growth was 9.1% in MedSurg and Neurotechnology and 5.9% in Orthopaedics, with higher procedure volumes supporting demand.

Adjusted operating margin expanded 60 basis points to 26.2%, and adjusted EPS increased 10.3% to $3.52. Reported EPS declined 11.7% to $1.96, reflecting charges excluded from the adjusted measure.

The results fell short of management’s expectations. Stryker reduced full-year organic sales-growth guidance to 6.5%-7.0% and adjusted EPS guidance to $14.80-$14.90, from $14.95-$15.10 previously.

On October 26th, it completed the acquisition of ZuriMED, adding the FiberLocker rotator-cuff augmentation system to its shoulder-care offering.

The quarterly dividend is $0.88, or $3.52 annualized. The increase announced in December 2025 extended the dividend-growth streak to 32 years; the company has not yet announced its next annual increase.

The payout is about 24% of our $14.85 adjusted EPS forecast. Procedure growth and margin improvement support further dividend capacity, while execution following the cyber incident and acquisition integration remain important risks.

Blue-Chip Stock #3: Caterpillar Inc. (CAT)

  • Dividend History: 33 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 20.2%

Caterpillar manufactures construction and mining equipment, engines, industrial turbines, and locomotives, and also provides financing through Cat Financial.

Its dealer network, installed equipment base, and aftermarket service relationships help sustain customer ties across economic cycles.

On October 29th, 2026, Caterpillar reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Sales and revenues increased 17% to $20.7 billion on higher volume and favorable pricing.

Construction Industries sales increased 18%, Resource Industries grew 15%, and Power & Energy rose 18%. Data-center investment continues to support demand for large engines, turbines, and related services.

Adjusted operating margin expanded to 20.6% from 17.5%, and adjusted EPS increased to $7.20 from $4.95. Reported EPS was $7.13.

Operating cash flow was $4.4 billion, while record backlog of $78.1 billion provides visibility into future demand. Capacity constraints, manufacturing costs, and tariffs can still limit how quickly orders translate into earnings.

Caterpillar returned about $2.0 billion through dividends and repurchases during the quarter. The quarterly dividend is $1.63, or $6.52 annually, following 33 consecutive years of higher annual payments.

That dividend is about 24% of our $27.17 adjusted EPS forecast. The return case combines earnings growth, dividend income, and potential valuation improvement, but equipment demand and customer capital spending remain cyclical.

Blue-Chip Stock #2: FactSet Research Systems (FDS)

  • Dividend History: 27 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 20.9%

FactSet Research Systems supplies financial data, analytics, and workflow tools to investment managers, banks, wealth managers, and other financial professionals.

Embedded workflows, proprietary data, and recurring subscriptions support customer retention and pricing power.

On September 30th, 2026, FactSet reported fiscal fourth-quarter results for the period ending August 31st, 2026. Revenue increased 6.3% to $634.7 million, while adjusted EPS rose 11.6% to $4.52.

Organic annual subscription value grew 7% to $2.57 billion, with retention above 95%. Adjusted operating margin slipped to 33.0% as compensation and technology spending increased.

Full-year adjusted EPS was $18.01, and free cash flow increased 14.6% to $707.5 million.

The November 2nd Hamilton Lane partnership expands private-markets data within FactSet’s portfolio analytics, supporting its push to connect public and private investment workflows.

At its November 10th Investor Day, FactSet reaffirmed fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guidance of $19.25-$19.65 and organic subscription-value growth of 5.0%-6.5%.

Its medium-term targets through fiscal 2029 include roughly 6% organic revenue growth, about 10% adjusted EPS growth, an adjusted operating margin of at least 36%, and average free-cash-flow conversion above 90% of adjusted net income.

The same day, FactSet announced a Carbon Arc partnership to add alternative data to its Workstation and AI ecosystem.

The quarterly dividend is $1.16, or $4.64 annualized, and FactSet has increased its dividend for 27 consecutive years.

Recurring revenue and cash generation support capital returns, while the investment case depends on converting AI and data investment into sustained subscription growth and margin recovery.

Blue-Chip Stock #1: Albemarle (ALB)

  • Dividend History: 30 years of consecutive increases
  • Expected Total Return: 26.5%

Albemarle is a global specialty-chemicals company and one of the world’s largest lithium producers.

Energy Storage supplies battery-grade lithium, while Specialties provides bromine-based and other products for industrial and consumer markets.

On November 4th, 2026, Albemarle reported third-quarter results for the period ending September 30th, 2026. Sales increased 18.8% to $1.55 billion as lithium pricing recovered and Specialties benefited from better pricing and volumes.

Adjusted EPS was $2.58, while adjusted EBITDA increased 186% to $646 million. The recovery remains closely tied to commodity prices rather than a steady progression in underlying demand alone.

Operating cash flow totaled $406 million, including a Talison dividend, and free cash flow was $325 million.

Albemarle delivered more than $150 million of year-to-date run-rate cost and productivity improvements. It raised its Specialties EBITDA outlook to $350-$375 million and reduced 2026 capital-spending guidance to $300-$350 million from about $500 million.

Talison’s CGP3 achieved 30 days of nameplate performance in September, a recovery milestone following the earlier fire.

Ragnar Udd is scheduled to become chief executive on February 1st, 2027, with Kent Masters moving to executive chairman.

The quarterly dividend is $0.41, or $1.64 annualized, following July’s 1.2% increase. Sure Analysis records 30 consecutive years of rising annual dividends.

The projected payout is modest, but lithium-price volatility and the substantial valuation-recovery assumption make its leading expected return especially sensitive to the commodity cycle.

Final Thoughts

These 10 companies combine at least a quarter-century of dividend growth with the highest Dividend Risk Score in our Sure Analysis Research Database.

Their records span very different economic environments, which is meaningful evidence of durable business models and shareholder-friendly capital allocation.

The return cases vary.

FactSet and Thomson Reuters provide recurring data and business-services revenue, while Stryker and Badger Meter offer exposure to medical technology and water infrastructure.

H2O America provides regulated water-utility exposure, while Caterpillar and Stepan provide industrial and materials exposure. Bank of Utica adds a strongly capitalized banking business, alongside greater liquidity and disclosure limitations.

Brown & Brown offers an asset-light insurance model, and Albemarle supplies more cyclical lithium-recovery potential.

We estimate that Albemarle has the highest five-year annual return potential, followed by FactSet and Caterpillar.

Those estimates should not be treated as guarantees, particularly where valuation recovery or commodity pricing contributes meaningfully to the outlook.

We also recommend you distinguish dividend safety from current yield, as several of the strongest businesses in this group offer modest starting income.

The best choice will therefore depend on whether you prioritize present income, dividend growth, business stability, or greater upside with more operating cyclicality.
