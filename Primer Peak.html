Springfield SA-35 4-Inch Review [2026]

November 17, 2026 Daniel Reedy Gun Review, Guns, Pistol, Pistol Reviews, Reviews, Semi Automatic Pistol
Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

The Browning Hi Power is one of the most legendary handguns of all time. From Axel Foley to Claire Valentine, SOE to FBI HRT, the Hi Power has found itself in the hands of legendary warriors and on-screen heroes for nearly 100 years. While production closed a few years ago, all hope was not in fact lost. Springfield Armory, among others, came to the rescue, raising the Hi Power from the ashes. Today we’re taking a look at Springfield’s take on the type, the SA-35. In particular we’re checking out the 4-inch variant, similar to the Argentinean-produced “Detective” variants of the 1970’s. How does this retro revival stack up to today’s offerings? Let’s take a look!

Design of the Springfield Armory SA-35

If you know the Hi Power, then you know the SA-35. This is an alloy frame, single action only (SAO), hammer fired pistol. Chambered in 9mm, it comes standard with a single 15-round magazine. In a change from the original, there is no magazine disconnect here, so the gun will fire with the magazine removed, and the magazines eject freely. I consider this to be an improvement, however I wish the gun came with more than one magazine. For those with classic guns, the SA-35 is compatible with original Hi Power magazines.

This version of the SA-35 features wooden grip panels, each held on with a single screw, with solid texturing going completely up the panel to help with a positive purchase on the gun. Make sure to add threadlocker to these screws, as I found my grip panels wobbling upon arrival, requiring tightening before shooting. Front and rear straps of the grip are smooth, as with the original Hi Power. The slide is a dream to manipulate, making loading, unloading, and stoppage clearance drills a pleasure.

Trigger

The Browning Hi Power is widely considered to have a somewhat poor trigger. This is something I’ve always taken issue with, as my original model has a fairly nice example. As good as that one is, the Springfield Armory SA-35 is even better. Over the past few months I’ve come to truly fall in love with the trigger of the SA-35. Travel is short, the break is fairly crisp, and the face is smooth and then, which is my preference in a trigger.

I’m not alone in my thoughts, and that’s a good thing. I’ve put the SA-35 in the hands of several shooters on the range to get their thoughts. Each one has specifically called out the trigger as being particularly pleasant to press. Good job Springfield, this is what I’m looking for in a single-action trigger.

Controls on the Springfield Armory SA-35

Controls are solid on the SA-35. Our safety is slightly enlarged compared to the original Hi Power. While not quite up to modern 1911 standards, it does provide a decent ledge for manipulations with our thumb. There isn’t an audible “click”, but I can slightly feel when the safety changes positions. I would love to have a more positive safety, but it is an improvement from the originals. Southpaws will note that the safety is righty-only.

The magazine release button and slide lock lever are fairly standard fare for those familiar with the Hi Power. I typically manipulate the slide lock with my support hand when reloading, and find it easy to access and manipulate. There is a slightly sharp edge on the rear of the slide lock lever. I have never had an issue with this during manipulations, but it has snagged on my Milt Sparks Summer Special on occasion.

Sights

Sights are substantially improved from my original Hi Power. The front and rear are modern, dovetailed into the slide. Our front post features a white dot, while the rear is flat black with serrations to cut glare. The rear sight features a U-notch, which is not my preference, but overall I’m pleased with the sights on the SA-35. Careful observers will note the ledge on the rear sight, aiding with one-handed manipulations.

Range Time with the Springfield Armory SA-35

With only one magazine included with the SA-35, our time was limited to standard practice sessions. Over the course of a few months I put 816 rounds through the pistol. Below is a breakdown of rounds fired.

  • 1x Blazer Brass 115gr FMJ
  • 15x Remington UMC 115gr FMJ
  • 35x Winchester White Box 115gr FMJ
  • 115x Federal American Eagle 115gr FMJ
  • 177x Super Vel 124gr FMJ
  • 416x Hot Brass 124gr FMJ
  • 17x Federal HST 124gr
  • 20x Federal Punch 124gr JHP
  • 20x Federal HST 147gr JHP

My very first shots with the SA-35 were with a cold Five Yard Roundup, dropping only a single point. I followed that with a 276/300 on the Advanced Super Test. This was a great start, and that trend continued throughout the review process. With the irons I was able to keep rounds within a 2-inch circle at 10 yards offhand, and well within a B-8(c) at 25 yards. I imagine the gun is capable of better accuracy when using a rest and higher quality ammunition, but it’s certainly meeting my abilities from the start. Shooting from retention and various stages of the presentation proved to be no issue for the SA-35, running through 100+ rounds from non-traditional positions.

Recoil is a little more sharp than my Glock 48, but overall reasonable for a gun of this weight. I received a small amount of hammer bite when shooting the SA-35, which is a common result from Hi Power pattern pistols. The first range session created a small blister on my thumb. This never moved past the point of a small red mark in subsequent range trips. Occasionally I’d grasp over the beavertail when drawing from strong side, which I imagine would be less of a factor if shooting from AIWB due to the differences in angle. Slide bite was never an issue, even with a modern grip.

Under Pressure

I traditionally ride my support hand thumb on the side of the slide with my pistols. In the past this has proven to be no issue. When really crushing down on my grip I found that this would impede the travel of the slide on the Springfield SA-35. Pressing strongly on the slide stop lever could also cause issues with cycling. Once I moved my support thumb onto the frame of the pistol, these stoppages cleared up. If you are experiencing issues with your gun, take a look at your hand placement, and see if some changes need to be made.

Outside of this, I experienced zero stoppages throughout the review process with the SA-35.

Closing Thoughts on the Springfield Armory SA-35

Overall I really enjoyed my time with the Springfield Armory SA-35. I love my original Hi Power, and having a modern version gave me some ncie quality of life upgrades while also freeing me of the concern of damaging a piece of history. While there are some minor personal preference changes I’d make to the SA-35, I’m very happy with the gun. I’d love to purchase this sample, but my financial responsibilities as of this writing make that unfeasible. If you’re interested in picking up a Hi Power, consider the Springfield Armory SA-35.
