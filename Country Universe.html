Every No. 1 Single of the 2000s: Blake Shelton, “Some Beach”

November 18, 2026 Kevin John Coyne 2004, 2005, Every #1 Country Single, The 2000s 1



“Some Beach”

Blake Shelton

 Written by Rory Feek and Paul Overstreet

Radio & Records

#1 (4 weeks)

December 17, 2004 – January 7, 2005

Billboard

#1 (4 weeks)

December 25, 2004 – January 15, 2005

Blake Shelton’s vocal talent gets this one over the finish line,

Easily one of the best male singers of his underwhelming era, he’s able to keep “Some Beach” from veering into novelty territory by delivering the song with his tongue in cheek behind his gritted teeth.

I’ve never been able to hear the pun the song is built around. I know the profanity that “some beach” is supposed to sound like, but it starts with “sunnava” this far north. The song still works fine without it, and the payoff that his bad day culminates at the dentist ends things on a genuinely funny note.

Shelton’s chart topping days are just getting started, and this is an early example of how he was able to adapt the nineties country formula and make it work for him as the genre changed around him.

“Some Beach” gets a B.

    I always respected Blake Shelton for early hits that were out of left field even if I didn’t necessarily love them. This was the last of that stretch of singles before his career began to follow a more paint-by-numbers track.

    “Some Beach” was a novelty track masterclass, cleverly put together and with humor that holds up even for repeated listens, all coupled with a beat that proved impossible to get out of my ear. As a fellow Yankee, I also required a bit of adjustment before I was on board for the shorthand of “some beach” without the “of a”, but I was going out with a girl born and raised in Texas at the time and she assured me the shorthand was entirely culturally acceptable.

    Blake Shelton wouldn’t be entirely averse to quirky songs moving forward but they typically suffered from Peach Picker-era immaturity and predictability, and were always sandwiched in between the trend-hopping hits of his most successful era. I’ll always be partial to his risk-taking early era where he leaned in to his colorful persona for memorably quirky songs that not just anybody would have the guts to record and peddle to conservative country radio.

    Grade: A-
