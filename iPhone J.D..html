Review: Apple HomeView

November 18, 2026 by Jeff Richardson

The HomeView is a delightful, useful, and, dare I say, very cute new device from Apple. It works especially well in a kitchen where friends and family always seem to congregate, but it can also be useful in a living room, on a desk, or even mounted on a wall. My wife and I have been using one since the first day it went on sale (Friday, November 13, 2026). Since this is the first version of a brand-new device, it is no surprise there is room for improvement. But fortunately, Apple can easily add new features and fix shortcomings through software updates. Overall, I think that Apple has a real winner on its hands, and considering that these devices are still on backorder, there seems to be a decent amount of interest.

The hardware

I’m very impressed by the design of the HomeView.

Because I read early reviews of the HomeView before I took mine out of the box, I knew that it would be smaller than I expected. And yet I was still surprised when I saw it in person. The 7" diagonal display is just over 5" wide and tall. As a result, it doesn’t take up much space wherever you put it. But despite the small size, the screen is more than big enough for its intended purposes. Plus, it is a bright and beautiful display.

The touch response on the screen is perfect, presumably because Apple has been making touch screens for a long time with the iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch, etc. The hinge makes it easy to tilt the screen into position, but it also has enough friction that the screen never slips while I’m touching it.

I like that the power adapter that comes with the HomeView has the USB-C jack on the bottom. That way, when you plug it into a wall in your kitchen, the cord doesn’t protrude into your living space.

The device needs to be plugged in to work, so you need to find a place where it isn’t too awkward to run a cord to an outlet. And if you don’t currently have such a space, I am confident third-party developers will soon start selling different types of bases with batteries that can be charged overnight, so you can move the HomeView freely during the day. (Here is one example: the RooHaven.)

The best HomePod for a common area

Apple took everything it learned from the HomePod and made it better by adding a screen.

Like any HomePod, the HomeView has speakers, it is easy to set up, and it works incredibly well with the Apple ecosystem. If you already have an iPhone, the HomeView will be a great speaker for you.

And it sounds really good! Perhaps because of the bass speaker built into the stand, music from a HomeView sounds much better than music from one of my HomePod minis. In my living room, where I have a pair of HomePod minis on opposite sides of the room, I like the stereo separation. But the HomeView does such a good job of filling my kitchen with music that I don’t miss having two distinct speakers on the opposite sides of my head.

I don’t think that all HomePods need screens. If a HomePod is in a corner of a room and out of the way, it doesn’t need a screen. But for a HomePod in a central area where people gather—such as a kitchen island or counter—adding a screen vastly improves the HomePod experience. Selecting music on a HomePod using your voice works fine, but a screen makes it easier to select songs and playlists.

Apple has long supported a hand off feature on the HomePod: hold your iPhone near the top of a HomePod until the iPhone vibrates, tap transfer (if prompted), and the song playing on your iPhone transfers to a HomePod. But this feature works 10x better on a HomeView because of the visual indications that you get on both your iPhone and the HomeView. Thus, if I am listening to a song or podcast on my iPhone and I walk into my kitchen, I can just move my iPhone close to the HomeView and the audio will “jump” to the HomeView.

Like any HomePod, the HomeView works great with AirPlay. The other night, my wife was making some homemade cranberry sauce for Thanksgiving, since we are hosting my family at our house this year, and I was helping her in the kitchen. (To be clear, I have no cooking skills, whereas my wife is a professionally trained former pastry chef. The “help” I provide is doing dishes, grabbing things from high shelves, and … when I am lucky … tasting things.) I started playing a new album on the HomeView, then swiped down from the top right to see the Control Panel (a gesture that we all know from the iPhone), and shared the music to the HomePod minis in our living room and dining room. Suddenly, the music played throughout the downstairs, and it was nice to listen as we talked and moved from the early stages of the cranberries to the end stage of pouring the sauce into jars.

In addition to sounding good, music also looks good on a HomeView because the device displays album art (or, if you prefer, lyrics). And because the small screen is still much larger than an iPhone screen, it seems almost decadent for the album art to look this good.

A HomeView is the perfect HomePod for a common area.

Digital photo frame

I’ve never owned a digital photo frame, but I understand why they are so popular. It is fun to see great pictures of your family and friends, and when the picture changes from time to time, you are often surprised and delighted. One of the many faces on a HomeView is a Photos face, and it works great.

It works similarly to choosing a photo or photos for an iPhone’s lock screen. You can select specific people, pets, nature, cities, photo albums, etc. I created one Photos face that rotates between pictures that my iPhone randomly selects of my two kids, and another Photos face that rotates between pictures of my dog. It is fun to see a bright screen showing off a picture of one of my kids that I haven’t seen in a while whenever I walk into the kitchen.

Although I really like the Photos face, I would be remiss if I did not also mention the wonderful Snoopy face. Whenever the HomeView sees you looking at it, Snoopy comes on the screen and does something funny. I have huge respect for the artists who create these clever animations. They almost always bring a smile to my face.

Timer

Timers are very useful in a kitchen. I typically use my iPhone or Apple Watch to start a timer, and that certainly works fine. But I vastly prefer the timer on the HomeView because it is so big. I frequently find myself wondering how much time is left, and a quick glance at the HomeView’s screen is the best way to answer that question.

You can also create multiple timers.

Recipes

If a recipe is on a webpage, the HomeView can typically do an amazing job showing it. It knows how to read these webpages to extract a big image of the item you are cooking, the ingredient list, and the step-by-step directions.

This past Sunday, I came downstairs in the morning to the incredible smell of banana bread in the oven. My wife had asked Siri on the HomeView to find the banana bread recipe that she likes from a website called Simply Recipes. Tapping the ingredients button told her what to assemble. Then tapping the Start Cooking button provided all of the steps: one step on each screen; swipe right-to-left to move forward. If a step involves a timer, tap those words in the directions to start the timer for that exact amount of time.

My wife tells me this worked much better than the other Apple devices she has used in the kitchen. Sometimes she uses her laptop, but it takes up a lot of counter space, and she has to worry about flour or other ingredients getting into the keyboard. Sometimes she uses an iPhone, but the screen is small and frequently locks, so she has to unlock it to see the next step. And even though the iPad screen is technically larger than a HomeView screen, because the recipe is perfectly formatted for the HomeView screen, with just one step on the screen at a time, she said the instructions end up being even bigger and easier to read on the HomeView. My wife considers the HomeView the best of all worlds for reading a recipe: it doesn’t take up much counter space, and yet the screen is big and bright enough to be very useful. That said, she noted it would be more convenient if it were portable so she could move it around without worrying about the cord plugged into an outlet.

The HomeView isn’t perfect for recipes because it is designed for recipes on popular cooking websites. Over the years, my wife has downloaded countless recipes as PDF files, which she stores in iCloud. The HomeView lacks a Files app. As a workaround, you can use the Safari app on the HomeView to go to iCloud.com to see your Files (and Notes and Photos and more), but iCloud.com is not (currently) optimized for the HomeView’s square screen, so it is not an ideal experience. Plus, that cool recipe feature—extracting the ingredients, breaking it into steps—only works with a recipe on a website, not with a PDF file. That said, my wife gets many of her recipes from cooking websites, so over the last few days, I frequently heard her ask Siri on the HomeView to go to the XYZ website and look for the recipe for such-and-such. Sometimes, she would then tell Siri to look for a different one on that website until Siri finally found the one she preferred.

Just yesterday, Stephen Robles figured out one solution and created an iPhone app called HomeNom. Use that app to select a recipe from a PDF file or even text from a note. The app uses AI to figure out the ingredients and steps and creates a private website for that recipe. Then hold your iPhone next to the HomeView, and that special website jumps from your iPhone to the Safari app on your HomeView. And viola! You have the recipe on a website, so you can tap the HomeView’s recipe button to take advantage of a recipe perfectly formatted for the HomeView. Bravo to Stephen for this clever solution.

If you like to cook, I think that you will like the HomeView. And even if (like me) you don’t cook, if you find that the HomeView helps your spouse make something delicious, then everybody wins.

Add a loved one to your kitchen (or other common area)

The HomeView supports FaceTime. At first, I didn’t think this would all that useful of a feature. We can already use an iPhone, iPad, computer, and Apple TV (when paired with an iPhone) to do FaceTime calls. Does it really add much to also have FaceTime on a HomeView?

Yes. It does.

My daughter is currently away from home at college, so we often FaceTime to see her and catch up. So far, we have mostly used the Apple TV for FaceTime. It is nice to see her on a big screen while we sit on the couch. Or sometimes, I use an iPhone and hold it in my hand while she and I talk.

FaceTime on a HomeView in a kitchen is a very different experience. Having her face on a screen on the kitchen counter is much more like her just being another person hanging out in the kitchen with us and chatting. It is more natural to do other things in the kitchen (like preparing dinner) while we chat with her. It feels more informal and casual. I really like it.

I’m not saying that we will always use the HomeView when we want to have a FaceTime call with someone. But just like FaceTime on an Apple TV (with a beautiful large screen) is a very different experience from FaceTime on an iPhone or iPad (where you can easily move around to different rooms, but you mostly feel the need to stare at the screen), FaceTime on a HomeView is yet another very different and enjoyable way to use FaceTime. Apple did a great job with this one.

Security camera and smart home controls

A HomeView is designed to integrate with your smart home. You can configure a screen to show buttons of different sizes. You can tap a button to turn lights, fans, etc. on or off. Lights respond instantly. It works well.

Having said that, I’m not sure how useful this is for me. I already have HomePods, my iPhone, my Apple Watch, and other devices that respond to my voice, and I find it even easier to ask Siri to turn a light on or off rather than swiping the HomeView to bring up a screen with device control. Because the HomeView supports Siri, I now have one more device that I can talk to, but I already had other devices. If you don’t currently have any HomePods in your home, and if you don’t have an iPhone, Apple Watch, or other Apple device with you, then it will be nice that you can use your HomeView to control your HomeKit- or Matter-enabled accessories.

On the other hand, I do see the utility in using a HomeView to see a camera, such as one that shows the front of your house so you can see whether a package has been delivered to your front porch yet. Especially if you set up one of your screens so that the camera fills the entire screen.

In my home, this feature is limited. First, I use Eufy cameras, and I don’t currently have them configured for HomeKit because enabling that support downscales the video quality from 2K to 1080p. To try it out anyway, I’m using some free software on my Mac called Homebridge, which provides HomeKit support for virtually any camera, but it is not very user-friendly to configure, so I don’t recommend this solution unless you are really geeky. (It would be nice if there were some way to have a Eufy app on a HomeView.) Second, my Eufy cameras are battery-powered, and I don’t want the battery drain that would result from my always or frequently using my HomeView to show the camera. But if you have a HomeKit- or Matter-compatible camera with a dedicated power source, a HomeView is a perfect way to watch video streamed from your home camera.

I know some people will buy the version of HomeView without a base, designed to mount on a wall, so they can use it 24/7 as a smart home control panel. I have no doubt that it would do this job very well for them. So far, for me, the smart home features are nice additions, but nothing all that special.

Multi-user

The HomeView recognizes your face and your voice. Thus, I can ask Siri on the HomeView a question about something personal to me—something about my calendar, a question that can be answered by getting information in a text message to me, etc.—and other people in my house can do the same thing.

These are early days

I am amazed at how quickly the HomeView has become such a useful and delightful device in my home. Many of the core functions work really well.

But it is not all perfect. It is wonderful that the HomeView supports the more advanced version of Siri that you can also use on an iPhone running iOS 27. Both hearing the responses and seeing the results on the screen work well. But as I’m sure you know, Siri AI is still far from perfect and often doesn’t give me the answer I’m looking for unless I ask a second time. (To be fair, that’s often true for my kids, too.) That said, I have no doubt Siri will keep improving over time, and as it does, HomeView will too.

We are also in the very early days for HomeView apps. Apps that work with CarPlay typically work well on a HomeView. The Overcast app that I use for listening to podcasts works really well. I was even able to listen to a deposition transcript using the wonderful TranscriptPad app, not that I expect to listen to many depositions in my kitchen. The CarPlay support is a neat initial trick, but I hope that there will soon be a way to add more apps. I mentioned above that I want to see a Eufy app for a better experience seeing my cameras. And it is not just third-party apps. Why is there no Photos app? Why is there no Apple News app, an app that even includes a recipes feature that would seem to be perfect for a HomeView in a kitchen? Why is there a Messages app but no Mail app? You can play a YouTube video in Safari, but why is there no YouTube app if you just want to listen to something in the background and occasionally glance at the video on the screen?

There are also some curious omissions and things that still seem rather half-baked. Why can’t I rearrange the screens on a HomeView the way that you can on an Apple Watch? As John Gruber of Daring Fireball noted, why does Safari on the HomeView let you select text but not do anything with that selection?

I understand. These are early days. What matters is that the hardware is already excellent, and the software can improve over time. Indeed, it took Apple a while to figure out what made the iPhone, Apple Watch, and iPad special. With lots of HomeViews in lots of homes, Apple can learn from the feedback, and I trust that Apple will do so.

Conclusion

If you are on the fence about the HomeView, I encourage you to get one. No, this is not a revolutionary device like the iPhone. It just does a few things. But it does them well, it is useful, and it is fun. If you are already in the Apple ecosystem, a HomeView integrates really well with your other Apple devices. Kudos to the smart folks at Apple for getting so much right in this first version. And I’m already looking forward to the 2.0 version of the software.
