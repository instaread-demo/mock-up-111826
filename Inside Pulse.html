4K UHD Review: The Deadly Spawn

Joe Corey

Horror movies are a great way to start of a career. Mostly we see this as a great way to kick start a journey to be a major director or an actor. But it’s also the perfect way to establish a career in making monsters and gruesome special effects. If a special effects wizard can scare audiences on a shoestring budget, people will be eager to see how they bring the frights when they have a bit of money behind them. Most low budget monster films do their best to hide the fearsome critter until they absolutely have to show something since they fear disappointing viewers. This approach became popular after Jaws when Spielberg didn’t show the whole shark for a long while. He did it because the mechanical shark kept having issues and they couldn’t wait. What sets The Deadly Spawn apart from The Jaws clones that we see the monster and it looks astounding in its toothy glory. The film also seems a response to another Spielberg film.

Two campers in the woods of New Jersey witness some sort of object from space crash nearby causing a bit of fire. When they leave the tent to investigate, they unfortunately find some sort of space monster has hitched a ride onto Earth. The creature takes refuge the basement of a nearby house. The parents there are in the process of going off for the weekend although they decided to check to see if the basement is flooded after a storm. This is a bad idea. But nobody notices their screams because the youngest son Charles (Charles George Hildebrandt) is watching a monster movie on the TV with the volume blasting. Eventually Charles sneaks into the basement to scare an electrician. Except this doesn’t quite happen as planned. Charles sees this massive creature that’s a worm with massive sets of teeth ready to chow down on anything nearby. Is he about to become the next victim of this invader from another planet? Or will his skills acquired from being a Monster Kid save him from his parents’ fate?

The Deadly Spawn is a wonder to behold as an indie monster movie. It has aged well since it came out in 1983. The most important part of the film is the monster with a glisten on its teeth as it voraciously eats the human cast. What’s completely amazing is the budget on the film was $25,000. Even adjusted for inflation that’s $84,573 which is still impressively low for a feature film shot on 16mm film. How can the budget be so small? A lot of people worked on this as a labor of love. The love shows on 4K UHD instead of strings or zippers on the special effects. There was plenty of fine talent that created the scares. Special Effects Director John Dods went on to work on numerous films including Alien: Resurrection and The X-Files. He even went on to Broadway where he worked on Disney’s Beauty And The Beast, Mel Brooks’ Young Frankenstein and appropriately The Toxic Avenger Musical, based on the other great New Jersey horror. The Deadly Spawn is a fun and savage film that give us the hard truth about what would really happen if E.T. showed up in your house.

The Video is 1.37:1 full frame. The transfer is a new 4K restoration from the uncut 16mm camera negative. I’ve never seen it look this good. The Audio features the Theatrical Mono mix and the Original Mono in DTS-HD MA 2.0. You’ll hear the monsters munch away. The movie is subtitled in English.

Audio Commentaries include one with writer/producer Ted A. Bohus, a second with Bohus with Editor Marc Harwood, and a third with writer/director Doug McKeown, Actor Charles Hildebrandt, Special Effects Director John Dods, Executive producer Tim Hildebrandt and Production Assistant Tim Sullivan. All aspects of The Deadly Spawn are enlighted with these commentaries.

A Visit with John Dods and the Deadly Spawn (8:39) is visit to his workshop space from what appears the ’80s. We see his artwork and models. Plus we get to see the Spawn critters. Dods has a great baseball cap.

The Deadly Spawn – Comic Prequel Sampler (7:30) is a comic panel version of what happened before the film started.

Original Audition Tapes (35:59) is black and white video of the actors seeing if they can act with each other and special effects.

Alternate Opening Sequence (4:23) has a few different moments as the campers find the crash site.

Behind-the-Scenes Footage (4:56) has the puppeteers working the toothy critters. The creature effects are brilliant. This film really knew what would keep the people watching it for decades.

TV Spot and Theatrical Trailer (2:24) has the film titled: Return of the Aliens: The Deadly Spawn. There’s a lot of teeth and blood in the red band trailer.

Still Gallery has over 60 images of behind the scenes shots, press photos special effects tests and posters.

Synapse Films present The Deadly Spawn. Directed by Douglas McKeown. Screenplay by Douglas McKeown. Starring Charles George Hildebrandt, Tom De Franco, Richard Lee Porter, Jean Tafler, Karen Tighe, Ethel Michelson, John Schmerling, James Brewster & Elissa Neil. Running Time: 82 minutes. Rating: Rated R. Release Date: November 24, 2026.
